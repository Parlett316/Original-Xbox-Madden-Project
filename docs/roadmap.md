# Roadmap and open work

Last updated 2026-11-05.

## In progress

### Progression: performance v2 shipped
Test 2 (2026-11-03) met two of three criteria clearly: production drives progression, and half the bottom-third starters declined. 99s went 5 → 6 → 11, against stock's 31 and v1's 8; all 11 are established stars who were already 96–99. Results: [findings §3](findings.md#3-progression-experiments).

- **Shipped 2026-11-03:** `performance` is `build_all.py`'s default and is in the overlay's `data/strmdata.db`. The stock-progression file (with the music) is kept in `patched/…/progression_stock/` for rollback.
- **If fewer 99s are wanted:** lower `GAIN_BY_GROUP[0]` (90+, now 0.5) or add a 95+ group. Test with the same procedure.
- **Watch over more seasons:** the league OVR mean is drifting down slowly (−0.1/season), and generated rookies have to replace the real players.

## Ready but not hardware-tested
- **17-game season: works on hardware** (2026-11-03, years 1 to 4, the full interconference rotation). `patches/season17.py`, the default in `build_all.py` since then (`--no-season17` opts out). Franchises need the build they were started with: the franchise tables are sized at creation. Test files: `patched/Madden NFL 09 (USA)/season17_test/`.
  - What it does: a 3-week preseason; 17 games over 18 weeks with a bye; playoffs in weeks 19–22 and the Pro Bowl in week 23. The Super Bowl box score can be viewed during Pro Bowl week, and then the season advances to the offseason.
  - Year 1 can be a real NFL season (`--schedule nflverse_schedule/games.csv`, from nflverse's nfldata; the 2026 schedule was checked on hardware). Years 2+ are the game's own generated schedule plus week 18: each AFC division against the NFC division it played two years earlier (the modern rule), computed by the XBE every year.
  - Changes needed: `template.dat` (schedules plus franchise tables sized for 22 weeks), `strmdata.db` (`OWFW` week 21 and `OACI`/`OFAC` week 17) and the XBE (offseason-start week 22 → 23; a cave that adds week 18 after the year-2+ generator).
  - The hardware tests, in order:
    1. week 18 rematched week 1;
    2. the Super Bowl locked with the Pro Bowl at week 22;
    3. it still locked with no Pro Bowl;
    4. the missing `OWFW` row was fixed and the season reached the offseason, but the Super Bowl stats couldn't be viewed;
    5. the Pro Bowl came back with the XBE week patch and everything worked;
    6. year 2 had 17 weeks: the generator deletes week 18;
    7. keeping week 18 through the generator hung "Advancing To Regular Season";
    8. inserting week 18 after the generator worked: 18 weeks, 17 games each, no repeats (save checked).
    9. years 2 and 3 were played through the Pro Bowl and a second offseason: year 3's save (`SEYR` 2) has 18 weeks, 272 scored games, no repeats, and week 18 against a different NFC division from that year's rotation.
    10. the preseason picker was removed: every year plays the data's 3 preseason weeks. Year 4's save (`SEYR` 3) has 18 weeks, 272 scored games and no repeats.
  - Next: test 7-team playoffs on hardware (below).

## Future work
Effort is a rough estimate. "Unknown" means nobody has looked yet, so the first step is research. Suggested order: classic-team photos, after 7-team playoffs pass on hardware.

### Modern NFL rules ([findings §8](findings.md#8-season-structure-and-playoffs-09))
- **Varied or selectable preseason.** Small to large. Every year now plays the same 3 preseason weeks from the data; restoring a 3-game picker would mean reworking its builder (`0x40a000`) and pick limits (`0x40456c`, `0x40459d`).
- **Modern kickoff: works on hardware** (2026-11-08), `build_all.py --modern-kickoff`. The playbooks in `GAMEDATA.dat` set both teams' spots and scripts, and a wait step holds everyone, CPU kickers included, until the receiving team touches the ball (`patches/kickoff_setup.py`). The XBE puts the ball on the 35, kickoff touchbacks on the 35 and a kick out of bounds on the receiving 40 (`patches/kickoff_spot.py`; findings §7). Still to do: a kick landing short of the receiving 20 (a new rule; the landing spot isn't found yet). The kicking team drifts about a yard toward the sidelines during the kicker's motion, visible only in replays.
- **Overtime rules.** Unknown. They have no table fields, so they're in gameplay code. The user plays 5-6 minute quarters, so a fixed 10-minute overtime is too long; prefer two-thirds of the quarter length, or the stock length if it already follows the quarter setting.
- **Varied kickoff times in generated week 18s.** Small to medium. The cave uses Sunday 1:00 for all 16 games and has 11 bytes free, so a Saturday and Sunday-night mix may need more cave space.
- **2027 schedule in year 1.** Very small: re-download `games.csv` after the NFL releases it (about May 2027) and rebuild.

### Classic teams
- **Real names: looked fine on hardware** (2026-11-04, test file below). `roster/classic_names.py` matches the 145 single-season classic teams to nflverse's open roster data (downloaded to `nflverse_rosters/`; Pro-Football-Reference blocks automated downloads).
  - EA changed every jersey number on these teams, so the match is a per-team assignment on position group, years pro, height, weight and age. A team's ages are used only when they agree with its age-free matches, since some teams have placeholder ages.
  - Results: 6,006 high, 550 medium, 381 low and 649 unmatched, out of 7,586 players. High and medium go into the game (`--apply`, `build_all.py --classic-names <csv>`); low and unmatched keep "position #number". A spot check of each team's top-rated players gives that season's stars.
  - Test file: `patched/Madden NFL 09 (USA)/classic_names_test/DB_TEAMS.dat` (2,585,792 bytes, the same size as the current one).
  - Custom players: `roster/custom_players.py` puts a name, size, skin and photo into an existing slot, using psxp 1856-2692 for the photo (2693+ is the game's generic-face pool). First use: the '63 Bears' RT #81, confirmed on hardware.
- **Photos.** Large: needs the photo-slot expansion below. nflverse has `headshot_url` for many players, mostly recent seasons.
- **Names for the 31 all-time teams** ("All 49ers"). Medium: they span many seasons, so matching is harder.
- **The remaining 1,030 players** (low or unmatched). Medium: accept lower match quality or find another source.

### Rosters and franchise
- **Separate photo slots for the modern roster**, so 2008 roster saves keep their own photos. Medium to large. `uis_plyr.dat`'s directory is full, and its header is preloaded in both qkl bundles (fixed size), so this needs the qkl regions rebuilt.
- **Edit franchise saves.** Loading works (2026-11-04): `--save-unlock` skips the signature check, see [formats](formats.md). Saving an edited franchise from the game and reloading it works too: the game writes new data and padding signatures and valid CRCs, and the edit survives offseason stages. Roster saves appear to use the same stream driver, but haven't been tried.
- **Jump a franchise to a later week: works on hardware** (2026-11-05, a year 2 save from week 1 to week 18: simmed the postseason, signed coaches, reached the offseason's balance sheet). `tools/save_jump.py --week N`. In xemu (year 1), a save jumped from week 1 to week 18 simmed week 18, seeded the playoffs, played every round and the Pro Bowl, and reached the Owner's Box offseason. A jumped save also survives being saved by the game, reloaded in a fresh boot and simmed on. The hardware save went on through the whole offseason to week 1 of year 3: 272 games, 17 per team, and the same interconference rotation and week 18 divisions as a real year 3 save (`Season3Works`). Open: on that run the offseason start and draft-pick signing took a minute or two (usually 10 to 15 s) and the preseason sim was very slow. On the same XBE, a save that was never jumped (`Season3Works`) entered the offseason in the usual time, so the XBE isn't the cause and the jumped save's state is the likely one. Why is unknown. One lead: `TPMN` had 532 rows at week 1 of year 3 (353 of them `SEWN` 22), against 304 to 334 at the end of real seasons. It only slows the game down, so it doesn't block tests that stop before the next season. See [findings §8](findings.md#8-season-structure-and-playoffs-09).
- **Jump a franchise to a later year.** Unknown, partly researched. Setting `SEAI.SEYR` and `SSYE` in a year 1 preseason save half-advances: the schedule is generated, but with the wrong rotation, and the week and season counts are never set (findings §8). A real year 2 preseason also differs in `FSCR`, `OTIS` and the stat tables. The first step is to find which step of the advance fails.
- **Optional fullback.** Unknown, new. A team should be able to carry 53 players with no fullback if it wants, as real teams do. The game enforces position minimums: leaving the preseason, it refused the 2026 Bears ("do not have enough fullbacks"), and "CPU Sign Player" couldn't fix it because no free agent is a fullback. The check counts players who can play: the Bears' only fullback was injured. Only the Chiefs have two, so this could block any team, and it applies at every position. The fix works in xemu: zero the fullback in `DRPS` and `PLPS` and lower the `POSG` running backs by one in `template.dat` ([findings §8](findings.md#8-season-structure-and-playoffs-09)). It's in the build as `--optional-fullback`, off by default. On hardware (2026-11-05), a fullback can be released, and in a played game a tight end filled in at fullback; the preseason advanced too. That build hard locked on a divide by zero when a team trimmed its roster, and an XBE fix took the same save through a full preseason and regular season in xemu; it isn't yet tested on hardware. CPU rosters after an offseason are still to check. A season can also stop on the minimums: a sim stopped at week 16 with the Browns, and "CPU Sign Player" let it continue. The first step is to find where the minimums live (a table or XBE code) and whether the CPU's roster, depth chart and game-day logic cope with no fullback. Giving each team a second fullback or keeping some in free agency would only work around the block.
- **Thin free-agent pool in the modern roster.** Small to medium, new. A new franchise has 232 free agents and none at K, P, FB or LOLB, with only 2 MLB, 3 ROLB, 3 LG and 3 RT, so an injured kicker has no replacement (seen on hardware, 2026-11-05). The pool fills slowly from offseason cuts: at week 1 of year 2 it had 446 free agents but still 0 K, 2 P and 4 FB, and a real year 3 save has 640, with 10 K, 11 P and 15 FB. EA's ratings site lists only rostered players, but `roster/madden26_superbowl.json` has 487 players whose names aren't in the Madden 27 file, including 8 K, 5 P, 2 FB and 7 SAM. `roster/pick_free_agents.py` picks 97 of them (`roster/free_agent_picks.csv` and `.json`; `build_all.py --free-agents`), leaving out retirements found on 2026-11-05 in nflverse's 2026 rosters, Wikipedia and news, though those lists are incomplete. Players 32 or older lose speed, agility and strength. Works on hardware (2026-11-05): a new franchise simmed a season into the offseason with CPU Sign Player handling roster holes, and Play Now and franchise games work. No existing player changes except the QB floor. In xemu at week 1, the pool has K 6, P 3, FB 1, LOLB 7, MLB 10, ROLB 6. Findings from that xemu run: CPU teams signed 36 of the 97 before week 1, averaging 54.6 players per team instead of 53.6, and the build's 80 overall cap doesn't last, because overalls have already moved by week 1 (18 added players above 80, Pratt 92, Wagner 89). At week 1 no underlying rating had changed and no unsigned free agent's overall had moved, while 817 players on teams had a new overall, so overall seems to be recalculated from ratings for players on a team. Team averages varied about as much as chance would give, with no clear link to coach fields; the code isn't traced. Whether coaches affect later progression is untested. Their ratings are a year old, and photos depend on free slots. Limits: the starting roster in `template.dat` has 102 free `PLAY` rows, the free-agent DB in `DB_TEAMS.dat` has 40 (236 of 276), so 97 needs that DB grown and its archive repacked, which is untested for this file. The franchise `PLAY` table (2,754 rows) grows every season, reaching 2,634 by the end of year 4, and what happens when it fills isn't known. Positions still short after that could get made-up low-rated depth. On hardware, the Browns' season could only continue once the user created a player, made him a punter and signed him.
- **QB awareness floor: built, not yet in a tested build** (2026-11-05). EA rates some mobile QBs far below their overall: Daniels 76 OVR with 53 awareness, Murray 77 with 59, Ward 73 with 58, Willis 72 with 61. `build_roster.py` now keeps a QB's awareness within 12 of his in-game overall (`QB_AWARENESS_GAP`). In the roster report, this changes `PAWR` for 21 QBs and nothing else: Daniels 61 to 69, Murray 65 to 70, Ward 64 to 67, Willis 66 to 67, plus backups and free agents such as Fields (58 to 67) and Lance (48 to 62). The user reports low awareness hurting Daniels in sims; whether awareness causes that isn't measured, so compare sim stats. It reaches the game with the next roster rebuild. More broadly, exact EA ratings or a blend could be tried as a test roster: the mapping also compresses speed on fast QBs (Daniels 91 becomes 80).
- **Any position, any number.** Small, researched 2026-11-05, nothing patched. Loaded rosters aren't limited: `PJEN` is 7 bits, and the modern roster already has 443 players outside 09's own range for their position, including #0, which no stock 09 player wears. Limits only apply in code: a table at `0x61ee18` (primary range per `PPOS`) and `0x61eec0` (second range) feeds `0x39d510` (numbers offered, called from the edit code at `0x14d337`) and `0x39d6c0` (range check). `0x39d700` picks a number for rookies (`0x3d0d0a`) and signings (`0x3b0ebc`): it keeps a player's number if it's free, otherwise it picks from ranges hardcoded in its own code, not the table. Plan: set the table to 0-99 for the edit screen; `0x39d700` needs its own patch if new players should vary too. #0 displays cleanly on the white uniform's back, front and sleeve on hardware (2026-11-05). To confirm on hardware: what the edit screen allows now.
- **Gameplay sliders and CPU tendencies.** Unknown, new idea: tune them from franchise reports, the way progression was tuned.
- **Long-run progression check.** Mostly sim time: 5+ seasons, to see whether the slow OVR drift above matters.
- **Coach contract lengths.** Small. They still come from the 2008 slot (Blough's ran one year).
- **Port 2005-style offseason progression** (`PYRA`), if in-season tuning isn't enough.
- **Throwback rosters from 07 and 08** (2006 and 2007 seasons). Lower priority (the user prefers the classic teams). Their PLAY layout is identical to 09's, so the roster is nearly a direct copy; their headshot files may come along too.
- **2005 and 06 rosters in 09** (2004 and 2005 seasons). Lower priority. These need field conversion (115 and 111 fields vs 110).

### Graphics and presentation
- **Real 3D faces from older games.** Unknown. 2005 has `PLYRFACE.DAT` and 09 has `plyrface.dat`; the formats haven't been compared.
- **Uniforms and logos for renamed or relocated teams** (the Commanders are the obvious case). Medium, using the texture tools we already have (TERF, MMAP). Team names are already modern; first survey which logos, uniforms and team text still look like 2008.

  - Team logos: entry = id + 1 in `uis_tmlo.dat` (64×64) and `UIS_TMLL.dat` (128×128). The modern team is 25 (entry 26) and the classic Redskins are 36 (entry 37, a separate image); entry 251 is a third copy, use unknown. "All Redskins" (TGID 208) is 25/25 in `TLGL`/`TLGS` in `template.dat` (3 DBs) and `strmdata.db`, so it showed the W; changing its `TLGS` in `DB_TEAMS.dat` alone did nothing on hardware. Fixed in those tables (hardware, 2026-11-04). `fe.qkl` and `game.qkl` don't preload logo entry 26.
  - Palette alpha matters: a first W with solid areas at alpha 248–253 looked grainy on hardware; rebuilt from a transparent PNG with alpha 255, it's clean (hardware, 2026-11-04).
  - Shared with the classic teams: the "REDSKINS" wordmark (`TFTL` 25, `uis_tmfn.dat` entry 26, and that archive is full) and the nickname image (`UIS_TIRN.dat`/`UIS_TIRL.dat` entry 26, the only one; how classic teams select it isn't traced).
  - Still 2008 on hardware: the stadium wall text ("WASHINGTON REDSKINS"), the small wall logo and the stadium scoreboard logo. Not traced yet. All the Redskins teams share `TMFL` 25 and `TSID` 26, so stadium art may be shared with them; `TMFL` may be the midfield logo (a guess from its name).

  - Helmets: done for Washington's white and burgundy uniforms and the training camp copies, working on hardware (2026-11-04). `build_all.py --commanders-logo`; details in [findings §9](findings.md#9-team-logos-and-uniforms-09). On hold at the user's request: alternates 281, 282, 285 and 286, which keep the old helmet; if they change, the user wants the W with the spear through it. Stadium art is also on hold.
- **2008 coaches on the Select Sides screen.** Small, not traced. It shows `WAS - J.Zorn` and `GB - M.McCarthy` while franchise mode has the 2026 staffs (seen in xemu, 2026-11-04, on files that match the hardware-tested ones).
- **Improved textures generally** (sharper uniforms, fields, menus). Unknown: the tools can replace textures, but larger ones may not fit the Xbox's memory or the file's slots. A single replaced texture would be a cheap first test.
- **Older stadiums and uniforms in 09.** Judged not realistic, not tested: art formats changed between years.
- **Commentary.** New names need recorded audio, and the announcers only have 2008-era names, but existing clips can be reused for a team ([findings §10](findings.md#10-commentary-team-names-09)).
- **2005's radio show (the Tony Bruno Show) and newspaper storylines.** Judged not realistic, not tested: they're game code plus audio and art formats, not data tables. The first step would be finding where 2005 stores the show's audio and what triggers each segment. Even if the audio can be read, 09 has no code to play it, so a real port is unlikely.
